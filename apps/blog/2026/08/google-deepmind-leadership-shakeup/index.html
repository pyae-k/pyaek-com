Google's DeepMind Shake-Up: Why Hassabis Stepped Aside

tech Aug 14, 2026 4 min read By Pyae Phyo Kyaw

On 5 August 2026, Google announced a major reshuffle of its AI leadership: Demis Hassabis stepped down as CEO of Google DeepMind to become its chair and Alphabet's chief scientist, while CTO Koray Kavukcuoglu took over day-to-day control [1][2]. The change comes as Google races to keep Gemini competitive with OpenAI and Anthropic — and as market data shows Gemini losing share [2][3].

Story at a glance

What: a leadership change at the top

Hassabis moves to a strategic role focused on AGI and AI governance, while Kavukcuoglu — previously CTO and chief AI architect — becomes SVP of Google DeepMind, reporting directly to Alphabet CEO Sundar Pichai [1][2]. Long-time Google scientist Jeff Dean left to co-found Discovery Loop, a public benefit corporation backed by Google [1].

Why it matters: Gemini is falling behind

Google's Gemini app has 950 million+ monthly users, but market data shows it losing share to ChatGPT and Claude in technical and scientific fields [1][3]. Time reports Gemini has "fallen behind models" from rivals [2]. The reshuffle separates scientific research from the commercial push needed to catch up [1][2].

Who: the new leadership

The key figures are Demis Hassabis (now chair and chief scientist), Koray Kavukcuoglu (SVP of DeepMind), Sundar Pichai (Alphabet CEO), and Jeff Dean (departed to Discovery Loop) [1][2].

What changes operationally

  • Kavukcuoglu oversees Gemini models, frontier research, and the Gemini app [1].
  • Safety and readiness teams shift reporting lines toward global affairs [1].
  • Hassabis keeps Isomorphic Labs (AI drug discovery) [1].

How: separating science from shipping

The reshuffle resolves a tension inside DeepMind: Hassabis is a scientist who wants to push AGI research, while Google needs to ship competitive products fast [1][2]. By making Hassabis chair and putting Kavukcuoglu in charge of operations, Google gets both — a long-term science vision and a short-term product sprint [1][2].

What next: Gemini 4 and the model race

The historical parallel

Google has reshuffled AI leadership before under competitive pressure — most notably after ChatGPT's launch in late 2022, when Google declared a "code red" and reorganised around Bard/Gemini [2]. The pattern: when a rival leapfrogs Google, leadership changes follow.

The future outlook

  • Gemini 4 ships: If the new structure accelerates Gemini 4 and it matches rivals on coding and reasoning, Google can claw back share [1][2].
  • Share keeps sliding: If Gemini 4 disappoints, the market share loss to ChatGPT and Claude continues [3].
  • AGI focus: Hassabis's new role signals Google is betting on long-horizon AGI research even as it races on products [1][2].

What to watch: Gemini 4's benchmarks, market share data, and whether the new structure actually speeds up shipping [1][2][3].

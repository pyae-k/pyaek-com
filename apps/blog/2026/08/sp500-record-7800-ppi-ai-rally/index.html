S&P 500 Hits Record 7,800 as Cooling PPI Fuels the AI Rally

finance Aug 14, 2026 4 min read By Pyae Phyo Kyaw

On 13 August 2026, the S&P 500 touched an intraday record of 7,816.7 after July producer prices came in flat, easing fears of another Federal Reserve rate hike [1]. The index closed at a fresh record near 7,748.5, while the Nasdaq rose 0.54% to 26,588.49 [2][3]. AI hardware stocks led the charge — Micron gained more than 5% — but after-hours trading delivered a reality check for several AI names [1][3].

Story at a glance

What: a record high on cooling inflation

July's Producer Price Index (PPI) was flat month over month, below expectations, and slowed to 4.7% year over year from 5.5% in June [1]. Core PPI rose just 0.2% month over month [1]. The data followed an in-line July CPI of 3.4% year over year, keeping September rate-hike odds near 45% [2][3].

Why it matters: rates drive the AI trade

AI stocks are long-duration assets — their value sits in profits expected years from now. When rate-hike fears ease, those future profits are worth more today, so cooling inflation is rocket fuel for the AI trade [1][3].

Who: the winners and the check

The day belonged to AI hardware: SK Hynix rose 5.87%, SanDisk 5.58%, Micron 5.22%, Marvell 5.03%, and Intel 4.90% [1]. But after the close, Coherent fell more than 8% despite beating estimates — a sign investors now demand above-expectation growth [3].

How: inflation data moves AI valuations

Wholesale prices feed into consumer prices, and both feed into the Fed's rate path. When PPI cools, markets bet the Fed can hold rates steady — or even cut — which lowers the discount rate applied to future earnings [1][3]. That is why a flat PPI print can lift a trillion-dollar tech sector.

What next: the September decision

The historical parallel

This mirrors the 2024 AI rally, when cooling inflation and rate-cut hopes carried the S&P 500 to repeated records. It also echoes the September 2024 Fed pivot, when a 50-basis-point cut briefly spiked risk assets before they faded — the "buy the rumour, sell the news" pattern [3].

The future outlook

  • Fed holds: With CPI in line and PPI cooling, a September hold is the base case; markets keep climbing [2][3].
  • Fed hikes: A hot August inflation print would revive hike fears and hit high-multiple AI stocks hardest [3].
  • Earnings reality check: After-hours moves like Coherent's 8% drop show investors now punish "good but not great" AI results [3].

What to watch: the September FOMC meeting, the next CPI and PPI prints, and whether AI earnings can keep beating raised expectations [1][3].
